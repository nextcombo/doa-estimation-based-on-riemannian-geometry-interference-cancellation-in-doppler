# DOA Estimation Based on Riemannian Geometry: Interference Cancellation in Doppler-Amplitude Direction Estimation [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Estimation of the Direction of Arrival (DoA) of a sound source is common in signal processing; it is an important step in many tasks such as source localization, beamforming, source separation, spectral sensing, and speech enhancement [1]. Despite its extensive research attention over the past decades, acoustic DoA estimation remains considered a challenging open problem. It remains an active research area especially in noisy and reverberant environments and when there are interference sources present.
Source localization, particularly DoA estimation, often uses beamforming to solve [2]. Over the years, numerous beamformers have been proposed for these tasks. One class of beamformers based on the manipulated response power (SRP) from the output of the beamformer. For example, considering the maximum likelihood criterion for a single source, compute the output power from all directions of the beamformer and identify the direction with the maximum likelihood criterion as the DoA. Another example is the minimum variance uncorrelated linear estimate (MVUE) beamformer [7], [8], [9] which was first introduced by Capon [10]. The MVUE beamformer extracts the DoA of each existing source while maintaining unit gain in their direction and minimizing the response from other directions. An important extension of the MVUE beamformer is the linearly constrained minimum variance (LCMV) beamformer [11] which minimizes the output power under multiple linear constraints and can also be used for DoA estimation [12]. Another line of beamformers derived from subspace methods is the one that identifies the subspace of desired sources, assumed to contain only a small portion of noise and interference sources. A prominent subspace method also used for DoA estimation is the Multi-Signal Classification (MUSIC) [13], [14], [15], [16].
A notable algorithm for source localization is the Direction of Arrival Power-Phase Transform (SRP-PHAT) proposed in [17]. In SRP-PHAT, phase transformation is used to normalize different frequencies, allowing only their phase information to be considered. When considering broadband signals, this allows for the fusion of different frequencies. A popular time-domain implementation of SRP-PHAT is the Generalized Cross-Correlation with Phase Transform (GCC-PHAT), which uses phase transformation to normalize each cross-correlation. In recent years, sparse signal recovery methods have been proposed for DoA estimation [19], [20], [21]. Specifically, a method for addressing the sparse recovery problem is the Sparse Bayesian Learning approach proposed in [22], which has also been applied to DoA estimation problems, such as [23], [24].
This paper investigates the DoA estimation problem in a reverberation room where the desired source is composed of interferers along the interfering sources. We assume that the desired source is constant activity, while the interferers are only intermittently active. The number, location, and activity times of the radiating sources are unknown. Therefore, it is also unknown whether they are identified as desired or interfered with. The power levels of different sources are also unknown, and in fact, the interferer may be stronger than the source with overlapping activity periods. Our goal is to estimate the DoA of the desired source when multiple interferers are simultaneously active.
This setup poses a significant challenge to existing methods that rely on maximum power, as estimating the DoA of the strongest source could lead to beams pointing in the direction of the interferers instead of the desired source. Moreover, these beams can mask the beams pointing towards the desired source.
We address this challenge from a geometrical perspective. Our approach relies on the commonly used beamformer to implicitly consider Euclidean geometry when processing sample correlation matrices. Therefore, due to the sample correlation matrix being an Hermitian positive definite (HPD) matrix, important geometrical information is not fully utilized. Instead, we propose a new beamforming design method based on the Riemann geometry of HPD matrix manifolds [25], [26], [27]. Specifically, we analyze received signals in short time windows and consider the Riemann average of the sample correlation matrices within these windows [25]. Then, we utilize specific spectral properties of the Riemann average. In [28], it is proven that the Riemann average of HPD matrices preserves shared spectral components and attenuates non-shared spectral components. Consequently, continuous activity of the desired source and sporadic activity of interference sources allow us to associate the desired source with shared spectral components and the interference source with non-shared spectral components. By combining the above content, we show that incorporating the Riemann average into the beamformer design leads to interference suppression, i.e., generating spatial spectral which inherently rejects beams pointing towards the interference source and retains beams pointing towards the desired source. The resulting spectral is then used to estimate the DoA of the desired source. Importantly, our method is applicable to a large number of beamformers for DoA estimation.
By integrating our Riemann method, we propose several new implementations of beamformers: Delay and Sum (DS) beamformer, subspace-based beamformer, and MVDR beamformer, as well as the Bayesian learning method for DoA estimation proposed in [23].
We observed that, despite the widespread use of the HPD structure of the signal correlation matrix in general array processing, especially for DoA estimation, it has not been fully exploited. Herein, we establish that by dividing the computation of the sample correlation matrix into short time intervals, it can be treated as the Euclidean average of the segment's sample correlation matrix. This finding allows us to introduce the Riemann geometry of the HPD matrix, particularly allowing us to introduce a relatively simple method for extending the use of the Riemann mean instead of the Euclidean mean, and demonstrate its multiple advantages. Secondly, we prove that the proposed method can naturally be applied to a wide range of DoA estimation methods using signal sample correlation matrices, and we show that it is computationally efficient because it typically does not change the order of complexity of the DoA estimation method. Thirdly, we theoretically analyze the proposed method and show that it leads to higher SIR values, thereby resulting in better DoA estimation accuracy in the case of DS beamformers. A new noise sensitivity analysis method is proposed. Fourthly, we empirically demonstrate conditions that lead to adverse effects including simultaneous active multiple interference sources. We show the applicability of our method to both classical and recent DoA estimation methods and demonstrate significant performance improvements achieved.
We conclude with three points of view. First, in [29] and [30], similar setups to ours were considered, including the expectation source with accompanying interference sources, but under the context of signal enhancement. In [29], single expectation sources and single interference sources were considered, while in [30], multiple expectation sources and multiple interference sources were taken into account. However, both works assumed that each source (expectation or interference) had at least one segment where it was the only active source. Additionally, in [30], it was assumed that the number of required sources and their activity patterns were known. Second, in the radar context, the Riemann geometry of Toeplitz HPD matrices was used in [31] and [32] for target detection by comparing the Riemann distance and thresholds. In a radar setting, [33] estimated the correlation matrix as a linear combination of its own correlation matrix, with weights based on the Riemann distance. Third, in this article, we demonstrated the design of a beamformer using the Riemann method to reject DoA estimation of interference sources. However, other applications, such as signal enhancement, could also benefit from suppressing the spectrum of interference sources.
Article illustrations
Conclusion We propose a design of a Riemann-based beamformer and DoA estimation method for interference suppression in reverberation environments. Specifically, the Riemann average is incorporated instead of the DoA estimation of the sample correlation matrix, which inherently rejects interference sources. Our analysis shows that the DS beamformer, based on the Riemann geometry of the HPD manifold, results in a higher output SIR than typical DS beamformers, which implicitly considers the Euclidean geometry. We extend our method to other beamformers, such as subspace-based beamformers and MVDR, as well as Bayesian learning methods, and experimental results demonstrate superior output SIR and better DoA estimation compared to their Euclidean counterparts.
